# Pack: decision memo

For memos that record a decision, or propose one. The danger here is not bad
prose. It is a decision that looks agreed when it was not.

## Configure your stakeholders

List who must have a stance block, by kind of decision.

| Decision area | Stakeholders who need a stance block |
|---|---|
| (example) Track A | Mira Okafor |
| (example) Track B | Dev Anand, Lio Brandt |
| (example) Anything about money or hiring | all partners |

## D1. Stance blocks present

Every stakeholder named for the decision area has a stance block. **HARD
FAIL** if one is missing.

A stance block takes exactly one of four forms:

1. **Verbatim quote**, with where and when it was said.
   `> "We should wait for the pilot data." (Mira Okafor, team chat, 2026-09-28)`
2. **Verbatim quote recorded as an open question**, when they asked rather
   than stated.
   `> "What happens to the Track B timeline if we do this?" (Dev Anand, call, 2026-09-29) [open question, unanswered]`
3. **"Deferred to owner"**, with the source of the deferral.
   `Deferred to owner. (Lio Brandt, email, 2026-09-30: "your call")`
4. **"Not yet captured"**, stated plainly.
   `Not yet captured.`

Anything else (a paraphrase, "broadly supportive", "aligned") is a finding.
**HARD FAIL** on a paraphrased stance.

## D2. Dissent kept verbatim

If anyone disagreed, their words appear as they said them, in the final memo.
A move from proposed to resolved that softens, summarises or drops a dissent is
a **HARD FAIL**.

## D3. Decision honestly labelled

"Proposed" stays proposed until the owner decides. A memo that says "we have
decided" with only "not yet captured" stances, or no owner sign-off, fails
core check 4 (honest classification).

## D4. Reversal cost named

The memo says what it would cost to undo the decision. Soft flag if absent.

## Verdict rule

A missing stance is `escalate`, never `rewrite`. The drafter cannot invent a
stakeholder's view. Only the owner can go and get it, or record "not yet
captured" on purpose.
